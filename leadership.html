Leadership Proposal

Creating a TA-Supported Course Resource Hub

Students should not have to struggle alone just because they are nervous to ask a question. This proposal applies my three key insights to make academic help less intimidating and more accessible in computer engineering courses.

01The Issue

One issue I have noticed as a student is that many students struggle with course material but do not always feel comfortable asking for help. They may be confused by a topic, behind on an assignment, or unsure how to study for an exam, but they are scared to attend office hours. Some students worry that they will ask a “stupid question,” interrupt the professor, or prove that they do not belong in the class. Others may think that a professor is intimidating, especially in a major-related course where the material already feels difficult.

Fear of Asking

Students avoid office hours because they worry about asking a “stupid question.”

Few Specialized Tutors

Upper-level courses have fewer tutors, and curricula change from semester to semester.

Limited SI Coverage

Supplemental Instruction helps, but it is not offered for every specialized class.

Tutoring can be helpful, but it does not always solve the problem. As courses become more specialized, there may be fewer tutors available who understand that specific course. The curriculum can also change from semester to semester. A tutor may have taken a class before, but the professor may now teach different topics, use different software, or assign a different type of project. Supplemental Instruction leaders can be especially helpful because they have usually taken the class before and are taking it again alongside the current students. This allows them to understand what students are learning at that moment. However, SI leaders are not available for every course, especially upper-level or specialized classes.

02The Solution

The result is that students can feel stuck between several support options that do not fully meet their needs. My proposed solution is to create a TA-supported course resource hub when I become a teaching assistant. This would be a professor-approved website or course page that gives students a clear, low-pressure place to find help. It would not replace the professor, office hours, tutoring, or SI. Instead, it would make those resources easier to use and give students several ways to get support based on what they are comfortable with.

The main goal of the resource hub would be to make help feel less intimidating and more accessible. Some students learn best by watching a short video. Others need to read a written walkthrough, work through practice problems, review common mistakes, or see an example step by step. Some students may not be ready to ask a question out loud but may be comfortable submitting it anonymously. Providing multiple ways to learn would recognize that students do not all learn or communicate in the same way.

course-hub / start-here

“Start Here” Page

What students are learning that week, why it matters, and the main terms or skills to understand before moving forward.

Review Videos & Walkthroughs

Short explanations of difficult concepts in plain language, focused on one topic, one homework skill, or one common mistake.

Practice Problems

Problems with explanations, because students need to understand why a solution works, not just the answer.

Common Mistakes & FAQ

Shared confusion explained once, in a general way, without identifying any student.

Anonymous Question Form

A way to ask without embarrassment, with answers posted on the hub, in a review session, or at the start of class.

“How to Ask for Help”

Example emails, how to prepare for office hours, and how to explain what you have already tried.

Resource & Referral Page

Office hours, tutoring, SI, the Student Success Center, accessibility, counseling and wellness, and other campus support.

Study Planning

Weekly checklists, deadline reminders, exam-prep suggestions, and help breaking large assignments into smaller steps.

Feedback Form

Students suggest topics to review and say which resources help them most.

All materials on the website would need to be approved by the professor. I would not post old exams, quizzes, slides, answer keys, or other class materials unless the instructor specifically allowed it. If the professor does not want certain materials shared, I could create my own general study guides, practice examples, concept reviews, or links to approved outside resources instead. This protects academic integrity while still giving students useful support.

03Rationale: Built on My Key Insights

KEY INSIGHT 01

Curiosity

Saying “I do not understand this yet” is where learning starts. The anonymous form and FAQ make that safe.

 KEY INSIGHT 02

Hands-On Learning

Practice problems and step-by-step walkthroughs let students actively use the material.

 KEY INSIGHT 03

Meeting People Where They Are

Multiple paths to support, because quiet students may not ask out loud.

This solution is connected to what I learned in EDLP 520 and through my work as a UNIV 101 Peer Leader. In EDLP 520, I learned that leadership and communication are not about giving everyone the same response. Students have different backgrounds, confidence levels, and ways of learning. As a Peer Leader, I learned that a quiet student may not be uninterested. Some students are comfortable speaking during class, while others only open up in a one-on-one conversation or after they have had more time to think. The resource hub would apply that same lesson to a future TA role. It would give students different paths to support instead of assuming that one office hour, one tutor, or one explanation will work for everyone.

My own learning experiences also support this recommendation. I learn best when I can work through an example, test something myself, make mistakes, and understand why a solution works. That is why the hub would include practice problems, explanations, and step-by-step walkthroughs instead of only a list of links. Students should have opportunities to actively use the material, not only reread notes and hope they remember everything later.

04Stakeholder Perspectives

Students

Need approachable and flexible support.

Professors

Need accurate information and control over their course materials.

Tutors & SI Leaders

Need resources that complement their work rather than duplicate it.

The TA

Needs a realistic system that can be updated without becoming overwhelming.

This proposal also considers the perspectives of other people involved. Students need approachable and flexible support. Professors need accurate information and control over their course materials. Tutors and SI leaders need resources that complement their work rather than duplicate it. A TA needs a realistic system that can be updated regularly without becoming overwhelming. The course resource hub would address these perspectives by using professor-approved content, clearly explaining its purpose, and directing students to the appropriate person or campus resource when a question is beyond the TA’s role.

05Detailed Plan

Phase 1 · Before the Semester

Align with the Professor

My implementation plan would begin before the semester starts. First, I would meet with the professor to discuss expectations, course policies, which materials may be shared, and the topics that students commonly struggle with.

Phase 2 · Weeks 1–2

Build the Foundation & Introduce It Early

During the first two weeks of the semester, I would create the basic structure: a welcome page, a list of official course resources, office-hour information, a question form, and a weekly schedule. I would introduce the site early so students know it exists before they are already struggling. I would make it clear that asking for help is normal and that the website is one option among many.

Phase 3 · Throughout the Semester

Update Weekly & Watch for Patterns

Throughout the semester, I would update the hub each week based on the current course material. I would add short concept reviews, practice questions, common mistakes, and answers to anonymous questions that could help the whole class. I would also watch for patterns. For example, if several students are confused about the same homework topic, I could create a short walkthrough or ask the professor whether it should be reviewed in class.

Phase 4 · Mid-Semester Checkpoints

Collect Anonymous Feedback

To evaluate whether the resource hub is helping, I would ask students for anonymous feedback several times during the semester. I would ask questions such as: Which resources did you use? Did the site make you more comfortable asking for help? Which topic still needs a clearer explanation? I would also track general patterns, such as which pages students use most often and which questions repeatedly appear. I would not use the website as a way to judge individual students. Its purpose would be to identify where the class as a whole needs more support.

Phase 5 · End of the Semester

Reflect, Refine & Share the Model

At the end of the semester, I would reflect on what worked and what needs improvement with the professor. If students found the resource hub useful, the structure could be reused and adapted for future semesters. Other TAs and instructors could also use a similar model for their own courses. The exact materials would change depending on the class, but the main idea would stay the same: students should not have to struggle alone just because they are nervous to ask a question.

06Evaluation at a Glance

Questions I Will Ask

  • Which resources did you use?
  • Did the site make you more comfortable asking for help?
  • Which topic still needs a clearer explanation?

Data I Will Track

  • Which pages students use most often
  • Which questions repeatedly appear
  • Class-wide patterns only, never individual judgments

Who Provides Input

  • Students, through anonymous and feedback forms
  • The professor, through approval and end-of-semester reflection

What Success Looks Like

  • Students report feeling more comfortable asking for help
  • Recurring confusion is addressed with targeted walkthroughs
  • The structure is reused in future semesters and by other TAs

07Looking Ahead

As a future TA, I want to be more than someone who grades assignments or repeats information from the professor. I want to be a resource that helps students feel less intimidated, more prepared, and more comfortable seeking help. My experience as a UNIV 101 Peer Leader taught me that students are more likely to succeed when they feel seen, supported, and able to ask questions without embarrassment. A TA-supported course resource hub would allow me to carry that same leadership approach into the computer engineering classroom.

Students should not have to struggle alone just because they are nervous to ask a question.
